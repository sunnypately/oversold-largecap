How the screen and fair value work

The screen

A stock is on the screen when its latest-quarter revenue is up at least 20% from a year earlier, it trades at least 25% below its 52-week high, and it is worth at least $10B — the Oversold Growth Monitor’s rule limited to large caps. The universe is the Russell 1000 plus an AI watchlist; pre-revenue names are left off. The list is ranked by the gap to fair value.

Fair value: three lenses, blended

Earnings (45%). Forward EPS times a fair P/E that depends on the business. Secular growers: 15 + 0.25 × revenue growth, bounded 15–45x. Semiconductor equipment: 15 + 0.2 × growth, capped at 28x for the equipment cycle. Servers and contract manufacturers: 14x. Memory and storage: valued on mid-cycle earnings rather than today’s peak. Power producers: 20x.

Sales (25%). Only for growth names without positive earnings. Fair price/sales = 2 + 0.12 × growth (capped at 100%), scaled by gross margin and current profitability; halved for asset-heavy AI cloud and data-center operators, with CoreWeave’s debt subtracted.

Street consensus (30%). The mean analyst price target.

Weights renormalize when a lens doesn’t apply. Upside = fair value ÷ live price − 1. Signals: at least 40% deep discount, at least 15% discount, −10% to 15% near fair, below −10% premium. Confidence is High when both earnings and street targets exist, Medium with any two lenses, Low with one.

Limits

Debt is mostly ignored. Peak-cycle earnings in memory, semiconductor equipment and hardware can make a stock look cheaper than it is, and much of this list rides the same AI spending cycle. Fair values are a starting point for research, not price targets, and not investment advice.

Refresh

Prices are live from Finnhub on page load and every 5 minutes while the page is open. The ticker list, fundamentals and fair values are rebuilt each NYSE trading day before the open; market holidays are skipped. This copy shows the list as of the date at the top until its data files are replaced.

Not on the screen

Large caps checked at the last refresh that grow revenue less than 20% a year, aren’t 25% off their high or lack the data to value them, plus growth-screen names just under $10B.
